Purpose

This document indexes constitutional standards in the NICE ecosystem.

Constitutional standards define what must remain invariant across all implementations.
They constrain platforms, interfaces, and governance structures without specifying how those constraints are implemented.

This index does not define standards.
It defines where standards belong, what boundary they govern, and what they explicitly do not cover.

⸻

What qualifies as a Constitutional Standard

A constitutional standard in NICE:
	•	Constrains authority, not behavior
	•	Defines boundaries, not mechanisms
	•	Applies regardless of platform, protocol, or vendor
	•	Cannot be bypassed by optimization, scale, or convenience

Constitutional standards are normative and non-optional.

⸻

Constitutional Standard Categories (v0)

The following categories are reserved for constitutional standards:
	1.	Human Authority & Personal Trust Boundary
	•	Origin of intent, consent, custody, authorization, and interpretive context
	•	Prohibitions on inferred intent, delegated authority, or anticipatory action
	2.	Declared Participation Mediation Boundary
	•	Evaluation of compatibility, eligibility, and visibility
	•	Separation of evaluation from execution
	3.	Governed Collective Participation Boundary
	•	Collective constraint enforcement without authority origination
	•	Structural inheritance and accountability requirements
	4.	Declaration Semantics
	•	What declarations are
	•	What declarations are not
	•	Inspection vs execution constraints
	5.	Invariants & Non-Goals
	•	Explicit rejection of agentic control
	•	Rejection of behavioral optimization
	•	Rejection of silent gatekeeping

⸻

Explicit Non-Coverage

Constitutional standards do not define:
	•	Protocols (e.g., DIDComm, blockchains)
	•	Data formats or schemas
	•	APIs or SDKs
	•	Performance, optimization, or routing behavior
	•	Platform internal architecture

Those concerns belong to interface standards or platform-specific documentation.

NICE Constitutional Standards Index (v0)

This index enumerates the standards that bind the NICE Constitutional System.
These standards define invariants and constraints, not implementations.

Constitutional standards:

• are normative
• are implementation-agnostic
• constrain what platforms may do
• do not prescribe protocols, data models, or code
• remain valid even as platforms evolve or are replaced

No constitutional standard authorizes execution, optimization, inference, or automation.

⸻

CS-1 — Authority Origination Standard

Boundary: Human Authority & Personal Trust

Defines:
• where authority may originate
• what constitutes valid intent, consent, custody, and authorization
• the prohibition on inferred or substituted authority

Explicitly excludes:
• delegation of authority to systems
• inferred goals or behavioral intent
• action ahead of human observation

⸻

CS-2 — Assistance Without Delegation Standard

Boundary: Human Authority & Personal Trust

Defines:
• constitutionally permitted interpretive assistance
• limits on sensemaking and guidance
• non-agentic posture requirements

Explicitly excludes:
• autonomous action
• decision substitution
• optimization or behavioral shaping

⸻

CS-3 — Declarative Participation Standard

Boundary: Declared Participation Mediation

Defines:
• what constitutes a declaration in NICE
• inspection-based evaluation semantics
• pull / observe semantics

Explicitly excludes:
• executable instructions
• push-based permission
• routing authority

⸻

CS-4 — Eligibility Evaluation Standard

Boundary: Declared Participation Mediation

Defines:
• eligibility reconciliation semantics
• compatibility vs permission distinction
• non-authoritative evaluation

Explicitly excludes:
• discretion
• prioritization
• enforcement actions

⸻

CS-5 — Participation Visibility Standard

Boundary: Declared Participation Mediation

Defines:
• declared availability and visibility
• notification without permission
• publication without routing

Explicitly excludes:
• execution paths
• invitations that imply obligation
• command dissemination

⸻

CS-6 — Commitment & Provenance Standard

Boundary: Declared Participation Mediation

Defines:
• commitment recording
• provenance guarantees
• accountability without control

Explicitly excludes:
• enforcement authority
• incentive structures
• retroactive constraint changes

⸻

CS-7 — Collective Governance Inheritance Standard

Boundary: Governed Collective Participation

Defines:
• constraint inheritance semantics
• cumulative narrowing of authority
• governance without empowerment

Explicitly excludes:
• autonomous collective authority
• override of human standing
