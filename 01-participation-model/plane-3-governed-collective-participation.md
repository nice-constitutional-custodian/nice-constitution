Plane 3 — Governed Collective Participation

Constitutional Plane — Plural

Purpose

This document defines how collective participation is governed in NICE without transferring authority away from humans or granting autonomous power to collectives.

This plane exists to answer one question only:

If participation is collective, under what declared constraints may it occur?

Collectives coordinate participation and enforce declared rules —
they do not originate authority, intent, consent, or discretion.

⸻

Role in the Constitutional System

Governed Collective Participation operates downstream of:
	•	Human Authority & Personal Trust, where authority originates, and
	•	Declared Participation Mediation, where compatibility is evaluated.

This plane is:
	•	plural by design,
	•	structurally constrained, and
	•	constitutionally subordinate to human authority.

Collectives exist to enable coordination at scale — not to absorb standing.

⸻

What a collective is (constitutionally)

In NICE, a collective is an intentional coordination structure, not a system, platform, or authority.

A collective:
	•	defines shared rules,
	•	constrains participation,
	•	and enforces declared conditions.

A collective does not:
	•	originate authority,
	•	infer intent,
	•	reinterpret consent,
	•	initiate execution independently, or
	•	act on behalf of humans without explicit declaration.

Participation remains human-rooted at all times.

⸻

Governance without empowerment

Collective governance in NICE is enforcement-oriented, not discretionary.

This means:
	•	rules are declared, not improvised,
	•	constraints are enforced, not silently negotiated,
	•	accountability exists without centralized control.

Governance ensures adherence to declared conditions; it does not grant new powers.

⸻

Inheritance is cumulative and narrowing

All collective participation inherits constraints from upstream constitutional planes.

Inheritance is:
	•	cumulative — all upstream constraints apply, and
	•	narrowing — no collective may relax or override inherited limits.

Collectives may only:
	•	add constraints,
	•	specialize scope, or
	•	further restrict participation.

They may never broaden authority.

⸻

Root collective types (canonical)

Governed collectives in NICE fall into explicit root categories, which are plural and extensible:
	•	Commons-of-Place
Coordination rooted in geography or locality.
	•	Commons-of-Practice
Coordination around shared work, discipline, or domain.
	•	Commons-of-Affinity
Coordination around shared identity, lived experience, or mutual concern.
	•	Commons-of-Identity
Coordination around identity primitives, credentials, and trust anchors.
	•	Commons-of-Sensemaking
Shared substrates for truth, interpretation, and understanding.
	•	Commons-of-Intent (Constitutional Commons)
Coordination around shared intent, principles, and boundaries — not outcomes.
	•	Ecosystem Commons
Large-scale, multi-layered commons spanning many sub-commons.
	•	Infrastructure Commons
Stewardship of physical substrate, execution guarantees, and fairness at the ground layer.

Each category:
	•	is governed, not empowered,
	•	inherits all upstream constraints, and
	•	remains accountable to human participants.

⸻

Collective participation is evaluated, not executed

Collective participation is evaluated constitutionally, but physically realized elsewhere.

In practice:
	•	participation is realized through humans acting in declared roles,
	•	under constraints enforced by collective governance,
	•	with no independent collective execution.

No collective originates action independently of human participation.

⸻

Accountability without autonomy

Governed collectives may:
	•	record commitments,
	•	maintain provenance,
	•	enforce compliance, and
	•	provide auditability.

They may not:
	•	act autonomously,
	•	conceal decision-making, or
	•	exercise discretionary authority.

Accountability must be explicit and inspectable — or it does not exist.

⸻

Constitutional invariant

Governed Collective Participation exists to ensure that:
	•	coordination does not become control,
	•	scale does not erase human standing,
	•	governance does not become autonomy, and
	•	collectives remain accountable to people.

Collectives coordinate participation —
they do not replace the participants.

Nothing more — and nothing less.
