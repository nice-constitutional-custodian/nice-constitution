Constitutional Participation Map

Purpose

This document provides the authoritative textual definition of the NICE Constitutional Participation Map.

It explains:
	•	where authority originates
	•	how participation is declared and reconciled
	•	how collective participation is governed
	•	what never occurs at the constitutional layer

This document is normative, not descriptive.
It defines constraints that all platforms, standards, and implementations must obey.

Overview

The NICE Constitutional Participation Model is organized into three constitutional planes.

Each plane has a distinct role.
Each plane is constrained by the one above it.
No plane executes actions.

The three planes are:
	1.	Human Authority & Personal Trust (singular)
	2.	Declared Participation Mediation (singular)
	3.	Governed Collective Participation (plural)

Together, these planes define how participation may occur without transferring authority away from humans.

⸻

Plane 1 — Human Authority & Personal Trust (Singular)

This plane defines the absolute origin of authority in NICE.

What originates here
	•	intent
	•	consent
	•	custody
	•	authorization
	•	interpretive context

Authority originates once — and only once — with the human.

No system, platform, collective, or process may:
	•	infer goals
	•	reinterpret intent
	•	initiate action
	•	act ahead of human observation
	•	override human standing

This plane establishes a singular constitutional trust boundary that all participation must pass through.

Nothing in NICE acts unless it is traceable to authority declared here.

⸻

Plane 2 — Declared Participation Mediation (Singular)

This plane defines how declared participation is evaluated without execution.

What happens here
	•	declarations are inspected
	•	compatibility is evaluated
	•	eligibility is reconciled
	•	permission scope is matched
	•	availability is published

What does not happen here
	•	no commands are issued
	•	no execution is routed
	•	no discretion is applied
	•	no authority is created or modified

This plane exists to reconcile what is declared — not to decide what should happen.

It is singular by design:
	•	one mediation plane
	•	many possible implementations
	•	zero authority transfer

⸻

Plane 3 — Governed Collective Participation (Plural)

This plane defines how collectives participate without becoming authorities.

Collectives are:
	•	governed, not empowered
	•	constrained, not autonomous
	•	structurally subordinate to human authority

Key properties
	•	collective rules inherit constraints from higher planes
	•	inheritance is cumulative and narrowing
	•	no collective originates authority independently of humans

Governed collectives are plural by design.

Root Commons Categories (Canonical)

Governed collective participation in NICE occurs only through constitutionally recognized Commons types.
Commons are intentional coordination structures — not generic networks, platforms, or organizations.

The following root categories are canonical:
	1.	Commons-of-Place
		Coordination rooted in geography or locality (e.g., neighborhoods, cities, regions).
	2.	Commons-of-Practice
		Coordination around shared work, discipline, or domain (e.g., healthcare, climate science, education).
	3.	Commons-of-Affinity
		Coordination around shared identity, lived experience, or mutual concern (e.g., families, cohorts, mutual aid groups).
	4.	Commons-of-Identity
		Coordination around identity primitives, credentials, and trust anchors — enabling selective disclosure, legitimacy, and standing.
	5.	Commons-of-Sensemaking
		Shared substrates for truth, interpretation, and understanding — supporting divergence without collapse and local context without fragmentation.
	6.	Commons-of-Intent (Constitutional Commons)
		Coordination around shared intent, principles, and boundaries — not outcomes — expressing collective plea and legitimacy.
	7.	Ecosystem Commons
		Large-scale, multi-layered commons composed of many sub-commons (e.g., industry or well-being ecosystems).
	8.	Infrastructure Commons
		Stewardship of physical substrate, mesh management, and execution guarantees — preventing invisible gatekeeping at the ground layer.

Status: Canon v2 (unless otherwise noted)

These categories are constitutionally distinct roots.

Any collective participation structure must map to one or more of these roots to be considered conforming.

New collective forms may be proposed, but require explicit constitutional amendment to introduce a new root category.

Arrow Semantics (Critical)

Arrows in the Constitutional Participation Map have one meaning only:

Constraint and permission propagation

They do not represent:
	•	execution
	•	routing
	•	messaging
	•	commands
	•	data flow

Evaluation vs. Execution
	•	Participation is evaluated constitutionally
	•	Execution is never constitutional
	•	Physical realization occurs only through humans acting via PERS in declared roles

No arrow implies action.

⸻

Collective Participation Clarification

Although collective participation is evaluated constitutionally:
	•	its physical realization always occurs through human participation
	•	humans act in roles appropriate to the collective
	•	no collective bypasses the human trust boundary

This distinction is fundamental and non-negotiable.

⸻

Constitutional Invariants

The participation model enforces the following invariants:
	•	Authority originates with humans — once, and only once
	•	Participation is declared, not inferred
	•	Mediation reconciles declarations; it never executes
	•	Collectives are governed without acquiring authority
	•	Evaluation and execution are never conflated

If any interpretation violates these invariants, it is non-conforming.

⸻

What This Model Is Not

This model does not define:
	•	platforms
	•	services
	•	protocols
	•	routing mechanisms
	•	optimization logic
	•	automation behavior

Those belong below the constitutional layer and must conform to it.
