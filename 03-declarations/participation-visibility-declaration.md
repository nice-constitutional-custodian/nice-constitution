Participation Visibility Declaration (v0)

Purpose

The Participation Visibility Declaration defines where, how, and under what conditions a bounded App (bApp) participation offering may be observed.

It answers the question:

Where can this participation offering be discovered or noticed?

This declaration exists to support discoverability without authority, enabling observation while preserving human agency and pull-based participation.

⸻

What This Declaration Declares

A Participation Visibility Declaration declares:
	•	That a specific bApp participation offering is visible
	•	The channels, contexts, or scopes in which it may be observed
	•	Any constraints on visibility, such as:
	•	Audience scope
	•	Role scope
	•	Contextual scope
	•	Temporal scope

Visibility is a declaration about observability, not readiness or permission.

⸻

What This Declaration Does Not Do

This declaration does not:
	•	Grant permission to participate
	•	Signal availability or readiness
	•	Determine eligibility
	•	Route participation or execution
	•	Notify or push participation offers to recipients
	•	Express intent by any human or collective
	•	Initiate any action

Visibility is not invitation, permission, or routing.

⸻

Relationship to Other Declarations

The Participation Visibility Declaration works in coordination with:
	•	Participation Availability Declaration
(whether the offering is currently open for consideration)
	•	Eligibility Declaration
(who may participate, if they choose to evaluate)
	•	Conformance Declaration
(whether implementations meet declared standards)

Visibility establishes where observation may occur, but does not imply that observation will occur.

⸻

Observation Semantics
	•	Visibility declarations are pulled or observed, never pushed
	•	They may be:
	•	Indexed
	•	Listed
	•	Queried
	•	Subscribed to
	•	Observation does not imply:
	•	Interest
	•	Consent
	•	Evaluation
	•	Obligation

No system may treat visibility as authority.

⸻

Constitutional Invariants

The following invariants apply:
	•	Visibility does not imply availability
	•	Visibility does not imply permission
	•	Visibility does not imply eligibility
	•	Visibility does not imply routing
	•	Visibility never transfers authority

A participation offering may be visible and never acted upon.

⸻

Example (Non-Normative)

A Commons steward may declare that a research bApp:
	•	Is visible within a specific Commons
	•	Is discoverable by humans holding a declared role
	•	Is listed in a shared registry or index

This declaration:
	•	Makes the offering observable
	•	Does not authorize participation
	•	Does not signal readiness
	•	Does not initiate execution

All subsequent action remains human-initiated and platform-mediated.

⸻

v0 Completion Criteria

This declaration is considered v0 complete when:
	•	Visibility is clearly distinguished from availability and eligibility
	•	No language implies push-based notification or invitation
	•	The declaration survives a hostile read without being interpreted as authority or routing
