Commitment & Provenance Declaration (v0)

Purpose

The Commitment & Provenance Declaration defines what commitments were made, what actions occurred, and what evidence exists to support accountability — without asserting authority, permission, or enforcement.

It answers the question:

What was committed to, what happened, and how can that be inspected later?

This declaration exists to support auditability and accountability without control, enabling after-the-fact inspection while preserving human agency.

⸻

What This Declaration Declares

A Commitment & Provenance Declaration declares:
	•	Declared commitments, such as:
	•	Participation terms
	•	Scope of action
	•	Duration or limits
	•	Observed outcomes, including:
	•	What actions occurred
	•	When they occurred
	•	Under what declared constraints
	•	Provenance references, such as:
	•	Attestations
	•	Receipts
	•	Logs
	•	Verifiable references to evidence

This declaration records what is accountable, not what is authorized.

⸻

What This Declaration Does Not Do

This declaration does not:
	•	Authorize participation or execution
	•	Grant permission retroactively
	•	Enforce compliance
	•	Interpret intent
	•	Judge correctness or success
	•	Assign blame or liability automatically

Provenance is not enforcement.

⸻

Relationship to Other Declarations

The Commitment & Provenance Declaration works in coordination with:
	•	Participation Declaration
(what participation was defined)
	•	Eligibility Declaration
(what compatibility was declared)
	•	Conformance Declaration
(what standards were claimed)

Commitment & provenance apply after participation has occurred, not before.

⸻

Observation and Inspection Semantics
	•	Commitment and provenance declarations are:
	•	Recorded
	•	Referenced
	•	Inspected
	•	Inspection may be:
	•	Human-led
	•	Tool-assisted
	•	Third-party reviewed
	•	Inspection does not imply:
	•	Enforcement
	•	Penalty
	•	Remediation
	•	Authority transfer

No system may treat provenance as automatic judgment.

⸻

Constitutional Invariants

The following invariants apply:
	•	Provenance does not imply permission
	•	Provenance does not imply correctness
	•	Provenance does not imply compliance
	•	Provenance never transfers authority
	•	Provenance supports accountability without control

Accountability exists without centralized enforcement.

⸻

Example (Non-Normative)

After a data-sharing bApp completes a participation cycle, a declaration may record:
	•	The declared scope of participation
	•	The actions that occurred
	•	Timestamps and references
	•	Attestations from involved systems

This declaration:
	•	Enables later review
	•	Supports dispute resolution
	•	Does not retroactively authorize actions
	•	Does not enforce outcomes

All judgments remain human-mediated.

⸻

v0 Completion Criteria

This declaration is considered v0 complete when:
	•	Commitments are clearly separated from permission
	•	Provenance is clearly separated from enforcement
	•	No language implies retroactive authority or control
	•	The declaration survives a hostile read without being interpreted as governance or execution logic
