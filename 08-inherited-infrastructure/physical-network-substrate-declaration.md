Physical Network Substrate Declaration

Inherited Infrastructure — Constitutional Constraint

Purpose

This declaration defines the constitutional constraints imposed by the physical network substrate on participation in the NICE ecosystem.

The physical network substrate includes the material realities beneath digital networking: cables, spectrum, towers, data centers, power access, right-of-way, and environmental conditions. These realities shape who can connect, who can be excluded, and who bears risk when systems fail.

Because these constraints precede all software, platforms, and protocols, they must be treated as constitutional conditions, not technical details.

⸻

Scope

This declaration applies to all physical infrastructure that enables network connectivity for NICE participation, including but not limited to:
	•	Physical transmission media (fiber, copper, wireless spectrum)
	•	Power availability and dependency
	•	Physical access points and interconnection facilities
	•	Environmental constraints (geography, climate, disaster exposure)
	•	Ownership and control of physical network assets

It applies regardless of jurisdiction, ownership model, or deployment scale.

⸻

Constitutional Principle

Physical infrastructure must not create invisible gatekeepers.

When control over physical connectivity is opaque, centralized, or unaccountable, human participation becomes contingent rather than sovereign. Constitutional protections must therefore extend down to the material layer.

⸻

Required Constitutional Properties

For a physical network substrate to be compatible with NICE participation, the following properties must hold.

1. Explicit Power and Ownership
	•	Control over physical network assets must be knowable and attributable.
	•	Ownership and operating authority must not be hidden behind abstraction layers.
	•	Participants must be able to understand who controls access and under what terms.

2. No Silent Exclusion
	•	Physical access must not be withdrawn, throttled, or conditioned without visibility.
	•	Exclusion mechanisms must be explicit, declared, and contestable.
	•	“Technical necessity” must not be used to mask discretionary exclusion.

3. Resilience as a Requirement
	•	Failure is assumed, not exceptional.
	•	Physical infrastructure must tolerate disruption, degradation, and partition.
	•	Participation must degrade gracefully rather than collapse catastrophically.

4. Environmental and Human Reality Awareness
	•	Physical networks must account for environmental limits, not deny them.
	•	Energy, climate, and geographic constraints must be acknowledged as real.
	•	Optimization must not externalize physical or human cost invisibly.

5. Non-Privileged Physical Position
	•	Physical proximity, ownership, or network centrality must not grant hidden authority.
	•	No participant may gain implicit governance power through infrastructure placement alone.
	•	Structural advantages must be surfaced and constrained.

⸻

Non-Authoritative Guardrails

The physical network substrate must not:
	•	embed policy enforcement without declaration,
	•	centralize control under the guise of efficiency,
	•	treat resilience as optional,
	•	or assume permanence or uniform availability.

Infrastructure supports participation — it does not define legitimacy.

⸻

Implications

This declaration implies that:
	•	Multiple physical substrates may coexist.
	•	No single physical network may be assumed canonical.
	•	Infrastructure may be shared, federated, or locally stewarded.
	•	Governance must compensate for physical asymmetries rather than ignore them.

⸻

Relationship to Other Constitutional Elements

This declaration constrains:
	•	Network & transport declarations (what assumptions are allowed)
	•	Governance boundaries (who may steward infrastructure)
	•	Participation eligibility (what dependencies are acceptable)

It does not define:
	•	mesh architectures,
	•	routing overlays,
	•	operational procedures,
	•	or infrastructure ownership models.

Those belong to future Infrastructure Commons standards and implementations.

⸻

Constitutional Invariant

Physical infrastructure must enable participation
without becoming its arbiter.

Connectivity may be constrained by reality —
but authority must never be.
