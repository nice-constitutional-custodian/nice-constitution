Physical Compute Declaration

Inherited Infrastructure — Constitutional Constraint

Purpose

This declaration defines the constitutional constraints imposed by physical compute environments in the NICE ecosystem.

Physical compute is where execution happens. Execution determines what can act, persist, observe, or fail. Because execution and custody directly affect human agency, physical compute is a constitutional concern, not merely an operational one.

This declaration specifies the conditions under which physical compute may be used without undermining NICE’s constitutional guarantees.

⸻

Scope

This declaration applies to all execution environments involved in NICE participation, including but not limited to:
	•	Personal devices
	•	Home or edge nodes
	•	Hosted or institutional compute
	•	Cloud or data-center environments
	•	Specialized execution hardware

It applies regardless of ownership, vendor, geography, or business model.

⸻

Constitutional Principle

Control matters more than location.

Physical proximity, decentralization, or performance alone do not guarantee human agency. What matters constitutionally is:
	•	who controls execution,
	•	who controls persistence,
	•	who can observe,
	•	who can exclude,
	•	and who bears responsibility when things fail.

⸻

Required Constitutional Properties

For physical compute to be compatible with NICE participation, the following properties must hold.

1. Explicit Control
	•	Execution authority must be explicit and inspectable.
	•	It must be clear who may initiate execution, under what declared conditions.
	•	Implicit execution triggers are not permitted.

2. Custody Legibility
	•	Custody of data, identity material, and execution artifacts must be legible.
	•	Humans must be able to determine where data resides and under whose control.
	•	Custody may be delegated only under explicit, revocable terms.

3. Bounded Execution
	•	Execution must occur only within declared, bounded environments.
	•	Execution environments must respect declared constraints on:
	•	scope,
	•	duration,
	•	isolation,
	•	and permitted actions.
	•	Compute may not exceed or reinterpret its declared envelope.

4. Human Override and Exit
	•	Humans must retain the ability to:
	•	halt execution,
	•	revoke authorization,
	•	withdraw data,
	•	and exit the environment.
	•	Exit must be practical, not merely theoretical.

5. Accountability and Attestation
	•	Execution environments must be capable of producing:
	•	receipts,
	•	attestations,
	•	and provenance artifacts.
	•	Accountability must be possible without granting enforcement power to the execution environment itself.

⸻

Non-Authoritative Guardrails

Physical compute environments must not:
	•	originate intent, goals, or decisions,
	•	infer or optimize human behavior,
	•	reinterpret declared constraints,
	•	act ahead of explicit authorization,
	•	retain custody beyond declared terms,
	•	or obscure responsibility through abstraction.

Execution environments act only under permission, never by discretion.

⸻

Implications

This declaration implies that:
	•	Centralized compute may be used only if governance, custody, and exit conditions are explicit.
	•	Distributed compute is not inherently safe if control is opaque.
	•	Performance, efficiency, or convenience do not override constitutional constraints.
	•	Execution that cannot be inspected, contested, or exited is constitutionally invalid.

⸻

Relationship to Other Constitutional Elements

This declaration constrains:
	•	Eligibility evaluation — execution venues must be eligible under these rules.
	•	Conformance assessment — environments must attest to these properties.
	•	Governed collective execution — collectives may execute only within compliant environments.

It does not define:
	•	platform architecture,
	•	runtime design,
	•	vendor requirements,
	•	or optimization strategies.

Those belong to standards and implementations.

⸻

Constitutional Invariant

Physical compute must not become a hidden source of authority.

Execution is permitted —
but control remains human.
