Commons Governance Boundary

Rules for Collective Participation & Execution Permission (Constitutional)

Purpose

The Commons Governance Boundary defines the constitutional surface where collective participation is governed under explicit, inherited rules — without generating authority, intent, or autonomous power.

This boundary ensures that collective action in NICE is:
	•	rule-bound,
	•	auditable,
	•	accountable,
	•	and structurally constrained,

while remaining subordinate to human authority and downstream of declaration mediation.

Collectives coordinate participation; they do not command it.

Constitutional Role

The Commons Governance Boundary:
	•	enforces declared participation rules,
	•	constrains execution conditions,
	•	applies inherited governance constraints,
	•	and requires accountability artifacts,

without:
	•	originating intent,
	•	granting authority,
	•	deciding outcomes,
	•	or overriding individual human standing.

Collectives are governed — not empowered.

⸻

What This Boundary Does

Within this boundary, a Commons:
	•	declares participation rules and constraints,
	•	enforces eligibility and scope inherited from upstream declarations,
	•	constrains how execution may occur,
	•	requires accountability artifacts (receipts, journals, provenance),
	•	provides explicit exit and dissolution paths.

Governance here is structural and explicit — never implicit.

⸻

What This Boundary Does Not Do

The Commons Governance Boundary never:
	•	creates human intent,
	•	initiates participation on behalf of humans,
	•	executes actions autonomously,
	•	overrides individual consent,
	•	infers goals or priorities,
	•	or accumulates discretionary power.

Collectives do not “decide for” participants.
They only constrain how participation is allowed once chosen.

⸻

Governance vs Execution

A core constitutional distinction:
	•	Governance is singular
Rules, constraints, and accountability surfaces are unified and mandatory.
	•	Execution may be plural
Multiple execution venues may exist, provided they conform to governance constraints.

Execution is always:
	•	downstream,
	•	bounded,
	•	revocable,
	•	and auditable.

⸻

Inheritance Model

All Commons inherit governance constraints from one or more constitutional root categories.

Inheritance is:
	•	structural, not optional,
	•	cumulative, not substitutive,
	•	narrowing, never relaxing.

No Commons may weaken inherited constraints.

⸻

Canonical Commons Root Types

The following root categories define governance inheritance, not operational behavior:
	•	Commons-of-Place
(coordination rooted in geography or locality)
	•	Commons-of-Practice
(coordination around shared work, discipline, or domain)
	•	Commons-of-Affinity
(coordination around shared identity, lived experience, or mutual concern)
	•	Commons-of-Identity
(coordination around identity primitives, credentials, and trust anchors)
	•	Commons-of-Sensemaking
(shared substrates for truth, interpretation, and understanding)
	•	Commons-of-Intent
(coordination around shared principles, boundaries, and collective plea — not outcomes)
	•	Ecosystem Commons
(large-scale, multi-layer coordination across many sub-commons)
	•	Infrastructure Commons
(stewardship of physical substrate and execution guarantees)

This taxonomy is plural and extensible, but inheritance rules are invariant.

⸻

Accountability Without Control

This boundary enforces accountability by requiring:
	•	declared rules,
	•	visible constraints,
	•	execution receipts,
	•	provenance records,
	•	and explicit exit paths.

Accountability does not imply enforcement power over humans.

Responsibility flows upward; authority does not.

⸻

Relationship to Human Authority

All collective participation remains subordinate to human authority.

No Commons originates authority.
No Commons overrides human standing.
No Commons participates independently of human-declared roles.

Collective governance exists only to constrain participation, not to compel it.

⸻

Core Constitutional Invariants
	•	Collectives are governed, not empowered.
	•	Governance enforces constraints; it does not generate authority.
	•	Execution may be plural; governance is singular.
	•	Inheritance is cumulative and narrowing.
	•	Exit is always available.
	•	Outcomes remain open.

⸻

Summary

The Commons Governance Boundary enables collective coordination without capture, without silent escalation, and without authority leakage.

It allows humans to act together at scale —
while preserving agency, accountability, and exit by design.
