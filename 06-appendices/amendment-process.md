AMENDMENT PROCESS
NICE Constitutional System — v0

This document defines how the NICE Constitution may be amended without erosion of human authority, silent drift, or retroactive reinterpretation.

Amendment is permitted.
Unbounded change is not.

⸻

	1.	Purpose

The amendment process exists to allow the NICE Constitution to evolve while preserving its core invariants.

It ensures that:
• Authority remains human-originated
• Constraints remain explicit and inspectable
• Changes are deliberate, reviewable, and reversible
• No implementation convenience silently reshapes constitutional meaning

Amendment is a constitutional act, not a development task.

⸻

	2.	What Constitutes a Constitutional Amendment

An amendment is any change that alters:

• The origin or handling of authority
• The meaning of a constitutional boundary
• The semantics of a declaration type
• The invariants governing participation, mediation, or governance
• The interpretation of terms defined in the Glossary

The following are explicitly constitutional changes:
• Renaming a constitutional plane or boundary
• Adding, removing, or redefining a declaration
• Changing “must not” constraints
• Introducing new authority-bearing concepts

The following are not constitutional changes:
• Platform implementations
• Interface protocols
• Execution mechanisms
• Optimization or inference techniques
• UI or developer tooling

⸻

	3.	Amendment Proposal Requirements

All amendments must be proposed as a discrete, reviewable artifact.

Each proposal must include:
• A clear statement of the change
• The motivation for the change
• The exact files affected
• The invariants preserved
• Any invariants intentionally modified

Every proposal must answer:
“What failure mode does this address, and what new failure mode does it risk?”

⸻

	4.	Prohibited Amendments

The following changes are constitutionally prohibited:

• Introducing non-human authority origins
• Allowing inferred intent, goals, or preferences
• Enabling execution without explicit authorization
• Collapsing evaluation and execution
• Introducing silent defaults or hidden delegation
• Allowing platforms to reinterpret declarations

Any proposal that violates these constraints is invalid by definition.

⸻

	5.	Review and Ratification

All constitutional amendments must undergo explicit review.

At minimum, review must include:
• A hostile-reader pass using the Hostile Reader Checklist
• A consistency pass against the Glossary and Terminology Decisions
• An explicit statement of backward compatibility or breakage

Ratification requires:
• Explicit acknowledgment of breaking changes (if any)
• Version increment according to VERSIONING.md
• Entry in CHANGELOG.md with rationale

Silence does not imply consent.

⸻

	6.	Versioning and Propagation

Constitutional amendments propagate through semantic versioning.

Rules:
• Patch versions: Clarifications with no semantic change
• Minor versions: Additive constraints or clarifications
• Major versions: Any change that alters interpretation, authority flow, or invariants

No platform or implementation may claim compliance across a major version boundary without explicit re-attestation.

⸻

	7.	Amendments vs Interpretations

Interpretation is not amendment.

Interpretations may:
• Clarify ambiguous language
• Provide examples
• Explain intent

Interpretations may not:
• Change constraints
• Introduce new authority
• Narrow or expand permissions

If interpretation changes behavior, it is an amendment.

⸻

	8.	Amendment Discipline

The Constitution is designed to resist convenience-driven change.

Amendments should be:
• Rare
• Explicit
• Defensible under hostile reading
• Anchored to human protection, not system efficiency

The burden of proof lies with the proposer.

⸻

	9.	Human Standing in Governance

Participation in constitutional governance is exercised by humans acting in their own right.

Individuals may participate while affiliated with organizations, collectives, or institutions, but no entity may substitute for, compel, or override human judgment.

No governance mechanism may require participants to act against their conscience, standing, or understanding as a condition of participation.

Legitimacy flows from human participation, not organizational endorsement.
